## Word length (room setting)
- The host picks **5, 6 or 7 letters** with the other room settings. Attempts are
  **8 / 9 / 10** respectively. Everything else (time per letter, hint, attack) is per
  position and needs no new numbers; the time ledger's maximum grows with the word
  (60 s for 6 letters, 70 s for 7).
- Answer lists grew (built 2026-09-15 by `scripts/build-words.mjs`, one file per language
  and length): English 1,300 / 1,000 / 900 for 5 / 6 / 7 letters; Spanish 1,060 / 1,000 /
  900. The Spanish 5-letter list stops short of the 1,300 asked for: past ~1,100 lemmas
  the frequency ranking only hands out archaisms and regionalisms, and *simple and commonly
  used* wins over the number. Every addition was reviewed by hand; what the review removed
  (names, places, nationalities, technical words, vulgarity, sensitive subjects, inflected
  forms and function words) lives in the script as `ES_EXCLUDE_V11` / `EN_EXCLUDE_V11`
  and their second pass, still accepted as guesses. Sources are downloaded into
  `scripts/sources/` (not committed).
- Mobile at 7 letters needs its own layout pass (tiles and keyboard at ~400 px).

## Hint (replaces the "letter + count" hint of `02`)
- One per player per round in the normal mode; **one per team per round** in team mode,
  spendable by any member. The reveal is marked on every teammate's board and keyboard.
  **Hints are anonymous** (decided 2026-09-15): the feed says "somebody used a hint" or
  "team X used its hint", never who.
- What it reveals depends on what the player (or team) already knows:
  - **Not every letter of the answer is known yet** (some position is neither green nor
    yellow): the hint reveals **a new letter in yellow** — "There is an R in the word" — from
    an unknown position, as today, without the count.
  - **Every letter is already known** (each position is green or charged yellow, and at
    least one is yellow): the hint **places one of the yellows**: "The R goes in position 3",
    marked green on the board.
- Time ledger as before: a hinted yellow is not paid; placing it later pays 5 s. A hinted
  green pays nothing.
- The hint button must be impossible to miss: large, pulsing until spent, with the count
  of hints left. The language of the words (Español / English) is shown in the game header,
  not only in the lobby.

## Teams
- A room setting: **normal** or **teams**. Two teams, any split — 1 vs 7, 2 vs 5, 4 vs 4
  are all fine. The host can assign; any player can also click a slot to move to the team
  they want. Teams are fixed when the game starts.
- **Moving between teams is a drag (2026-09-17).** In the lobby a player card can be
  dragged from one team's panel to the other: the host may drag anybody, everybody else
  only their own card. The arrow button on the card does exactly the same and stays the
  route for touch and for the keyboard, so nothing depends on a pointer drag. This is
  presentation only — no new event; the drop emits the `team:assign` (host moving
  somebody) or `team:join` (moving yourself) the button already emitted. **Team name and colour** are chosen in
  the lobby by the team's own members (assigned by default); the chat paints the team's
  tag with that colour.
- Every player keeps **their own board and their own keyboard** and types their own words.
  Teammates see each other's boards **live, letters and colours**; rivals see colours only,
  as today.
- **One clock per team.** It starts at the room's seconds. New letters add time to the
  team clock at the normal rates, but each answer position is **paid once per team**: if
  two teammates uncover the same letter, the second adds nothing. A rival team's solve
  takes **5 s off the team clock**, once. At 0 the whole team is done.
- **One solve per team.** The first teammate to solve solves for the team; the others stop
  (their boards freeze). A team that never solves scores 0 for the round.
- **Team chat** during the round: the team's own channel, live for every teammate from the
  first second, labelled "(Equipo)". Between rounds (results screen, waiting for the next)
  everybody talks in a room-wide channel labelled "(Todos)". Stickers are always global, in
  both channels and at every moment. Normal mode keeps the "finished only" text chat of the
  next section during the round and the same "(Todos)" channel between rounds.
- **Wins**: a round is won by the team with the most points in it. During the game the
  header shows only the **rounds** won, by team name ("Los Rápidos 1 · Búhos 2"). The
  **games won in this room** counter lives in the lobby — the room survives "play again",
  so it persists across games — and the host can reset it.
- **A player with no teammates sees no own-team panel (2026-09-17).** In a 1 vs N split
  the solo player's side has nothing to show — a panel listing only yourself is a card that
  says "you are alone". Their team clock and team score live where everybody's do, in the
  centre column, and the rival team keeps its panel.
- A disconnected teammate barely matters: the team clock keeps running, their board stays
  where it was, they can come back while the game lasts.
- **A team that walks out ends the game (decided 2026-09-17).** If every member of a
  team leaves the room on purpose — `room:leave`, or a kick, which counts as one —
  nobody is left to race, so the game does not drag on one-sided. The current round
  is closed exactly as it stands (a team that had already solved keeps its points;
  the other scores what the formula gives for the round it played, which for an
  unsolved word is 0), `round:end` goes out for that round and `game:end` right
  after it with the standings so far. The room is marked finished, so the host's
  "Jugar de nuevo" works from the results screen. Between rounds there is no round
  to score: the round that was about to start is cancelled and only `game:end` is
  sent. A **disconnection** never does this — an away teammate keeps their seat —
  and the normal mode is unaffected: there the round goes on without the leaver and
  the game only ends early when nobody is connected.

### Team scoring (`03` has the normal formula)
| Concept | Points |
|---|---|
| The team solves | **+40** |
| Team clock left when it solved | 1 point per 1 % of the initial time |
| **First** team to solve | **+20** (nothing for second) |
| Attempts of **every** teammate, after each one's first | **−4 each** (a big team pays for every word it sends) |
| The team still holds its hint at the end | +10 |
| The team does not solve | 0 |

Floor of 40 for a solve. The game ranking is the sum over rounds. The same −4 per attempt
(with a solve floor of 40) applies to the normal mode from v1.1 on; see `03`.

## Chat
- **One panel, not two (2026-09-17).** The live feed and the chat are the same panel: the
  round's system events (solves, penalties, hints, arrivals and departures), the stickers
  and the text messages share **one stream ordered by time**, with the composer pinned at
  the bottom and the sticker trigger beside it. There is no separate feed column any more.
  The **Equipo / Todos** switch appears above the composer **only in team mode and only
  during the round**; everywhere else there is a single channel and no switch. The same
  panel is what the results screen shows, and what the phone sheet shows in a compact
  layout. Somebody arriving gets their own line in the stream like any other event.
- **Normal mode**: during the round, text chat only for players who have **finished** it
  (solved, out of attempts or out of time), visible only to them; a player who finishes
  receives the **history of that round**. Everybody still playing sees stickers only. Between
  rounds everybody talks ("(Todos)").
- **Team mode**: see the team channel above; "finished" means the **team** finished.
- Kept per round and visible on the results screen; a new game starts a new chat.
- Limits: 200 characters, 1 message per second, and the offensive-word filter the word
  lists already use. `aria-live` on the message list.

## Observers
- Somebody who joins a room whose game is already running enters as an **observer**: they
  see the boards live (never the word before the round ends), read and write in the chat
  the finished players use (normal mode) or the "(Todos)" channel (team mode), and send
  stickers — everything they send carries an **observer** label.
- Observers wait in their own area of the lobby/game view. At the **next round** an observer
  may **take a free slot if they want to** (in team mode they pick or are assigned a team
  then); nobody is moved automatically, and while the room is full they keep observing until
  a slot frees. Their score counts from the round they join.
- The room holds at most **8 players plus 2 observers** (10 people). A third observer is
  refused.

## Sound
Nineteen cues (2026-09-17), replacing the four this document first promised. All of them
are **synthesised at runtime** — sine and triangle oscillators through a low-pass filter,
short attack/decay envelopes, peaks around **−14 dBFS** and most of them under **250 ms**
— so the game ships no audio files.

| Cue | When |
|---|---|
| `roomCreated` | the room is created |
| `playerJoined` / `playerLeft` | somebody enters or leaves the room |
| `gameStarted` | **every** round starts, not only the first |
| `tileReveal` | each tile flips when a guess is scored |
| `keyTap` | a key is pressed — **off** unless "keyboard sounds" is on |
| `invalidWord` | the word is not in the list, or the row is short |
| `letterFound` / `letterPlaced` | a new letter turns yellow / a yellow turns green |
| `solved` | you solve the word |
| `rivalSolved` + `penalty` | somebody else solves, and the −5 s lands on you |
| `timeWarning` | at 10 s and again on each of the last five seconds |
| `roundEnded` | the round closes |
| `gameWon` / `gameLost` | the final standings appear |
| `sticker` | a sticker arrives |
| `chatMessage` | a text message arrives — **others' only**, never your own |
| `hintUsed` | a hint is spent |

- The top bar carries the three controls together: **mute**, a **volume slider**
  (default **70 %**) and the **keyboard-sound toggle**. All three are persisted, so a
  player sets them once.
- Players still **cannot send sounds**: every cue is a consequence of something that
  happened in the game, never something anybody chose to play at somebody else.

## Room management
- The host can **kick** a player; the kicked player may rejoin after **30 s**.
- No "ready" gate: the host starts when they want (as today).
- The lobby shows how many are connected.
- **The QR is dropped (2026-09-17). The invitation is the room URL itself.** There is no
  QR image anywhere; the link to share is `/room/CODE` — literally what the address bar
  is already showing — and "Copiar enlace" ("Copy link") writes that URL to the clipboard.
- **Every room URL is an entry point (2026-09-17).** `/room/CODE`, `/game/CODE` and
  `/results/CODE` all behave the same for somebody who arrives from outside:
  - **no session** → the join view for that code (just the name field), not a redirect home;
  - **a session in another room** → the "one game at a time" card, with "leave it to join
    CODE" as the way out;
  - **a session that is dead** (the room is gone, or the seat is not there any more) → it
    is dropped **silently** and the join view is shown; no "sesión expirada" notice,
    because the player followed an invitation, they did not try to resume anything.
- `/?code=CODE` is kept for links shared before this change and **redirects** to
  `/room/CODE`.
- Mid-round disconnection, normal mode: the clock keeps running, the player may come back
  while the round lasts, and an absent player counts as not solved. (Closes the item that
  had been pending since 2026-09-11.)
- **What the client does while it is away (2026-09-17; the server's side of this is in
  `02-game-rules.md`).** The socket retries **forever** — there is no attempt limit and no
  point at which the client gives up and sends the player home. It also stops waiting when
  it has a reason to: it reconnects **immediately** when the tab becomes visible again
  (`visibilitychange`) or when the browser reports it is back `online`, instead of sitting
  out the backoff. Meanwhile the only thing on screen is a small **"Reconectando…"** pill —
  no modal, no toast, no navigation: the board, the clock and the chat stay exactly where
  they were, and when the socket comes back the pill disappears.

## Guess the phrase (a second game, mocked up 2026-09-15)
A different game on the same rooms, normal or teams, ES or EN. Mockup:
https://claude.ai/artifact/1u8BU1kHjJ5NNzBJh9XShL. Rules as drafted, to confirm before
building:
- A hidden phrase (long is fine, not absurd) shown as slots per word. **Nothing is
  given**: every letter, in every word however short, comes from the words the player
  types (decided 2026-09-15). The percentage of the phrase is over all its letters.
- Typed words must be real words of the room's length: the same allowed lists as Wordle.
- Phrase bank: about 300 per language, sayings and common expressions, 4 to 8 words and
  roughly 20 to 40 letters, no proper nouns, reviewed by hand like the word lists. The
  whole phrase is revealed to everybody when the round ends.
- The player types words of the room's length (5 / 6 / 7) on a Wordle-style board,
  **6 words** per round. No yellow: each letter of the typed word is **green** if it is
  anywhere in the phrase — and every occurrence of it in the phrase is revealed — or
  **grey** if it is not, and the key is disabled.
- **Time**: every occurrence revealed adds **2 s** (a phrase with five A's pays 10 s when
  A is found). **Points**: completing the phrase is worth **80** (harder than a word's
  40); the letters give only a small share, proportional to the **percentage of the phrase
  uncovered** (up to 20 at 100 %); then the order of completion, −4 per word sent and −5
  per failed phrase send.
- **Complete the phrase**: the FRASE key opens a modal with the known letters fixed; the
  player types the rest. **Up to 5 sends per round**, separate from the 6 words; each
  failed send costs **5 points**. Running out of words does **not** end the round: it ends
  only when the phrase is completed, the 5 sends are spent, or the clock runs out. On a
  miss the modal stays open with the result and the player decides whether to try again
  or close.
- The player's own clock sits in a side card (as the team clock does in team mode); the
  rivals list scrolls when the room is big, with the app's own thin scrollbar. On the
  phone, rivals are shown one at a time, rotating every few seconds.
- **Phrase, board and keyboard always fit on one screen and never scroll** — scrolling
  the board would be a handicap. Nothing else sits in that column: no captions; the
  "complete the phrase" action is a purple **FRASE** key at the end of the keyboard's last
  row (on the phone, a pill in the phrase card's header), and the modal carries the rule.
- Scoring follows the order in which players (or teams) complete the phrase, plus the
  share of the phrase uncovered. **No hint in this game** (proposed 2026-09-15, to confirm):
  revealing a letter is what every green already does, and the button only cluttered the
  header. The header is minimal: room code, round, language; in teams, the rounds score by
  team name.
- The chat panel exists in both modes (normal: text only among those who finished,
  stickers always; the round's events appear inside it as grey system lines). The chat
  input is one design everywhere: text field ("Escribe un mensaje…"), sticker button, send
  button; when text is not allowed the field is simply disabled. The score card is the
  Wordle one: "if you complete now" with its rows and the big total while the phrase is
  open, "your round" with what counts (phrase uncovered · N %) once it is over. Captions speak in percentages ("56 % of the phrase completed"), not letter
  counts.
- What you see of the others, in both modes: **their phrase as slots**, green where they
  have found a letter, never which letter — how far they are, not what they know. Teams:
  phrase, clock and hint shared; team chat; teammates additionally see each other's
  **letters tried** (green in the phrase / grey out of it), not full boards.

### Implementation notes (2026-09-15)
- The score card of the mockup is the formula: **time left %** + **80** for the phrase −4
  per **every** word typed (teams: every member's words) −5 per failed send + the
  completion order (20 / 15 / 10; teams: +20 to the first team only). The completion
  bonus is the floor of a completed round, as Wordle's 40 is. Without the phrase: the
  uncovered share (up to 20 at 100 %) minus the failed sends, never below 0.
- Team mode shares the phrase, the clock and the five sends; a miss by any member spends
  one of the team's sends, and the fifth ends the round for the whole team.
- The phrase banks are curated inside `scripts/build-phrases.mjs` (299 ES / 285 EN on
  2026-09-15, 4–8 words, 16–42 letters, accents allowed in the source and stripped at
  runtime like the word lists). The hint is off in this game.

## Deferred: updating the server without breaking rounds
Rooms live in memory, so every deploy restarts the process and ends every game in
progress. Keeping games alive across a deploy (dump rooms to Redis or disk on shutdown,
restore on boot, clients reconnect) is wanted but **deferred to a later version**
(decided 2026-09-15). Until then: deploy when nobody is playing. Everything new in v1.1
should still be written so that its state is plain data that could be serialized later.
